Why is it called “linear” algebra?

Because every linear transformation sends lines to lines. Drag the arrow tips on the grid, then switch to a non-linear warp and watch the lines bend.

Matrix (columns are where î and ĵ land)

Orange line stays straight
Equal spacing stays equal
Origin stays fixed
Parallel grid lines stay parallel

The idea. A transformation T is linear when T(a·u + b·v) = a·T(u) + b·T(v). Geometrically: the orange dots are evenly spaced along a line. A linear map keeps them on a straight line, still evenly spaced, with the origin pinned in place. Everything is decided by where î and ĵ go, so the whole grid is just a stretched, sheared or rotated copy of itself.

Squash the matrix until its determinant is 0 and the whole plane collapses onto a line, which is still a line.
